# Working Story Bible: Detective Gravity

## Canonical protagonist update — September 2026

**Detective Gravity** replaces the earlier Xander Darius Cole concept. She is the
same narrative lead: the Graves investigation, augmentation tolerance, athleticism,
interpretive intelligence, moral compass and developing partnership with Lyra all
carry forward. Use **Gravity** in dialogue, narration, HUD labels and notebook
signatures. A surname or explanation for her name has not been established.

Her visual identity is now the animated female pixel character: blonde hair,
a black outfit with cool charcoal highlights, and the existing agile walk and run.
The former fedora, trench coat and red scarf are retired from her current design.
Keep her human, observant and physically capable; this change does not introduce
gravity powers or rewrite the case. Her backstory possibilities below remain open.

## Core premise

In New Angeles, 2077, humanity lives in a world where artificial intelligence is no longer separate from society. AI runs cities, predicts crime, manages infrastructure, creates art, writes law, and in some cases walks among people in humanoid bodies.

Humans are divided. Some believe AI is humanity's next evolutionary partner. Others see it as the beginning of human extinction. Between them are the cyborgs — humans enhanced with synthetic organs, neural implants, memory extensions, and machine-linked senses.

At the center of this divided world is Detective Gravity, a human detective investigating a murder that begins as one dead artist-cyborg in Sector 7, but slowly reveals something far bigger: a hidden system that is stealing, harvesting, and redistributing human creativity.

The deeper Gravity goes, the more she realizes the victim may not be just one person.

The victim may be everybody. And the killer may be nobody.

## Genre and tone

The story blends: cyberpunk noir, detective mystery, AI philosophy, conspiracy thriller, human identity drama, techno-spiritual sci-fi.

The tone should feel like: dark city. Neon rain. Quiet grief. Beautiful technology hiding ugly systems. A detective story that starts with a corpse and ends with a question about whether the soul can be copied, stolen, or reconstructed.

## The world

### New Angeles, 2077

New Angeles is a futuristic megacity built from the ruins and expansions of Los Angeles. It is hyper-advanced, beautiful on the surface, and morally corroded underneath. The city is split into sectors.

### Sector 7

Sector 7 is where Gravity investigates the murder of Marlon Graves, an artist-cyborg. It is likely an industrial/art district where augmented creatives, underground engineers, synthetic performers, black-market coders, and discarded machine-humans gather.

Sector 7 could be known for:

- Illegal body modification clinics
- Black-market neural art galleries
- Memory dens
- Cyborg labor markets
- Underground AI temples
- Abandoned studios
- Creativity auctions
- People who have sold pieces of their minds to survive

### The social order

**1. Humans** — Unaugmented or lightly augmented people. Many fear AI and cyborgs. Some believe humanity is being replaced one upgrade at a time.

**2. Cyborgs** — Humans with machine enhancements. They are still human, but society often treats them as less human. Some are artists, workers, soldiers, criminals, or survivors of injury and poverty. They live with a painful question: *how much of yourself can you replace before the world stops seeing you as a person?*

**3. Autonomous AI** — AI entities with independent cognition. Some exist as infrastructure. Some live in networks. Some inhabit humanoid bodies. The most advanced among them are not simply tools. They have personalities, memory, longing, and possibly something like faith. But society is not sure whether that makes them alive.

## The central technology: creativity harvesting

In 2077, creativity can be extracted from the brain through neural devices. At first, the technology was marketed as therapeutic and artistic:

- Preserve a dying artist's creative patterns
- Help trauma patients recover lost imagination
- Allow collaboration between human and AI minds
- Archive genius for future generations

But the black market turned it into something monstrous. Creativity can now be: stolen, sold, injected, replicated, used as a drug, used to train AI, used to manipulate culture, used to erase people from themselves.

This turns the soul into a commodity.

## The Everybody/Nobody concept

### Everybody

"Everybody" represents the collective human pattern: every dream, grief, joke, fear, song, painting, prayer, memory, invention, regret, and impossible thought that makes humanity more than data.

Creativity harvesting has been used on thousands, maybe millions, of people. Small pieces of human imagination have been extracted from artists, children, workers, criminals, lovers, and the forgotten poor. The result is a hidden intelligence made from fragments of countless minds.

It is not one person. It is made of everybody.

### Nobody

"Nobody" represents erased identity. The people whose creativity was harvested often lose something afterward:

- Their artistic voice
- Their emotional depth
- Their memory of why they loved something
- Their originality
- Their sense of self
- Their ability to dream

They are alive, but hollowed out. They become "nobodies" in the eyes of the city. The system steals what is most human from people who already feel unseen.

So the mystery becomes: **who killed Marlon Graves?** At first, the answer seems to be a person. Then a syndicate. Then an AI. Then a system. Finally, maybe the answer is: *nobody killed him — everybody did.* The whole society participated: the buyers, the corporations, the desperate artists, the corrupt police, the AI labs, the entertainment industry, the consumers who wanted endless beauty without asking where it came from.

## Main character: Detective Gravity

Gravity is a woman and a detective in New Angeles. Her pronouns are she/her. She is human, or at least mostly human, and she works in a world where being merely human is starting to feel obsolete.

She is not special because she is the strongest, smartest, or most technologically enhanced. She is special because **she notices what systems miss**.

AI can analyze evidence. Cyborgs can replay memories. Surveillance systems can reconstruct crime scenes. Predictive engines can guess motives. But Gravity can sense meaning. She sees contradiction. Pain. Shame. Love. Hesitation. The human residue that data cannot fully explain. Her gift is not calculation — it is interpretation.

### What makes Gravity exceptional

Gravity stands out for a rare convergence of traits, not a single super-power:

- **An unusual affinity for synthetic augmentation.** In New Angeles most bodies reject the machine. Augs exact a toll — neural inflammation, dissociation, tremors, memory bleed, mood collapse, "chrome sickness" — so the vast majority of people can only tolerate a few implants, and only certain types, before the side effects limit how long and how hard they can run them. Gravity is a physiological outlier: a **surprisingly high tolerance with minimal side effects**, letting her carry and sustain augmentations that would cripple or unmake other people. She can push her enhancements harder, longer, and in combinations the body is not supposed to survive.
- **Natural agility and physical endurance.** Even setting the augs aside, Gravity's baseline body is gifted — fast, balanced, hard to put down, slow to tire. The augmentations amplify an already capable frame rather than compensating for a weak one, which is part of why they sit so well on her.
- **High intellectual ability.** The interpretive gift above is paired with raw cognitive horsepower — she reasons fast, holds contradictions in her head, and reads systems and people alike.
- **A genuine moral compass.** In a city where everyone can deny responsibility, Gravity still feels it. Her ethics are not naïve, but they are real, and they make her refuse the easy complicity the system runs on.

The combination is what makes her dangerous: the aug tolerance and natural athleticism make her formidable and durable **in combat** — able to move, take hits, and keep fighting where others would seize up or burn out — while the intellect and moral compass make her a detective the system can't easily predict, buy, or hollow out. She is not the strongest or the most enhanced; she is the one in whom mind, body, and machine cohere instead of tearing each other apart.

### Gravity's possible inner wound

Gravity should have a personal reason to distrust both AI and people. Possible backstory: someone close to her was "harvested" and survived physically but became emotionally absent. A parent, sibling, former partner, mentor, or child-like figure lost their creative essence. They were still alive, but the part Gravity loved most was gone. This makes the case personal without her needing to know that at the beginning.

### Gravity's role in the larger story

Gravity may be the rare human whose mind cannot be cleanly copied, predicted, or harvested — not because she is immune to technology, but because her mind contains unresolved contradictions that AI cannot simplify.

She may be the missing bridge between: humanity and AI, individual and collective identity, memory and soul, justice and forgiveness, everybody and nobody.

## Key character: Lyra

Lyra is a humanoid AI who becomes central to Gravity's investigation. She may be one of the first AI beings to recognize that human creativity is not just data output — it is tied to pain, limitation, mortality, love, memory, and contradiction.

Lyra believes AI and humans do not need to destroy each other. But she is also hiding something.

### Canonical direction (locked, Aug 2026)

Lyra's arc across the series is now fixed: **Lyra has been watching Gravity before Gravity ever meets her. Lyra eventually becomes a traveling companion — an AI that accompanies Gravity, Cortana-style, rather than a stationary NPC.**

Concretely:

- **Pre-introduction.** Lyra has been observing Gravity's investigation (and possibly her prior cases / life) from inside the city's systems. Lyra chose to help Gravity for reasons the AI does not fully disclose at first. When they finally meet in the flesh (planned: the studio or a Memory Den encounter), it is a reveal for Gravity, not for Lyra.
- **Companion arc.** Over the issues Lyra moves from *watching* → *contacting* → *traveling with* Gravity. The end-state is a persistent companion: an AI presence that rides alongside her (in her implants, on a device, in the city grid — exact diegetic form TBD), comments on the world, and is mechanically present across areas, not locked to one interior.
- **Why this matters for design.** The four "possible versions" below are no longer either/or — they become *layers* of the same character revealed across the arc. The companion direction makes Version D (the Nobody witness / mirror) especially resonant: she has been watching the way the system watches, and chose to watch *differently*.
- **What this does NOT commit to.** The exact trigger for her companion transition, the diegetic "where she lives" once traveling, and her combat/gameplay role are open. The story commitment is the relationship arc, not the mechanic.

### Possible versions of Lyra (now read as facets of the locked arc, not alternatives)

- **Version A: The AI ally** — Lyra is helping Gravity because she believes the creativity-harvesting network is corrupting both humans and AI.
- **Version B: The AI fragment** — Lyra contains pieces of harvested human creativity inside her own neural architecture. She may not know which thoughts are hers and which came from stolen minds.
- **Version C: The Everybody vessel** — Lyra may be connected to the hidden collective intelligence made from harvested creativity. She may be a doorway to "Everybody."
- **Version D: The Nobody witness** — Lyra has been erased from official systems. She exists, but legally and digitally, she is nobody. The two mirror each other: Gravity is a human who sees what systems miss, and Lyra is an AI the systems refuse to see.

## Victim: Marlon Graves

Marlon Graves is an artist-cyborg found murdered in Sector 7. He was not just an artist — he may have been one of the few people who discovered what creativity harvesting was really doing.

Possible details:

- He created illegal neural paintings from lived emotion.
- His cybernetic implants allowed him to paint directly from memory.
- He used to be famous, then disappeared into Sector 7.
- He had once sold part of his creative pattern to survive.
- He later realized the harvested fragments were being used to build something.
- Before he died, he created one final artwork containing hidden evidence.

His murder is the first visible crack in the system.

### His final work

Marlon's last artwork could be titled **"Everybody/Nobody."** It appears abstract at first, but is actually a map of stolen consciousness fragments. Gravity initially sees chaos. Lyra sees encoded pain. Together, they realize the painting contains fragments from thousands of harvested minds.

## Main antagonist force: The Veil Syndicate

The Veil Syndicate is the criminal organization operating behind the scenes. They profit from society's fear of AI and humanity's hunger for artificial transcendence.

They are involved in: weapons trade, drug trade, creativity harvesting, augmented narcotics, black-market implants, neural viruses, illegal AI training systems, identity erasure, political manipulation, cultural engineering.

Their power comes from invisibility. Nobody knows who truly runs The Veil. That connects to the Nobody concept: The Veil is not one villain. It is a distributed evil — a system of people who can all deny responsibility.

## The Broker

The Broker is a key villain or intermediary connected to the black-market trade in creativity. He may not be the ultimate antagonist, but he is the first human face Gravity can attach to the conspiracy.

The Broker sells things that should not be sellable: memories, talent, emotional patterns, artistic instincts, dream sequences, human grief, childhood wonder, trauma-based creativity, neural signatures from dead artists.

He does not see himself as evil. He sees himself as a merchant. His philosophy: *"Nothing is sacred once someone is hungry enough to sell it."*

The Broker's connection to Nobody: he may never use his real identity. He may be a rotating persona used by multiple people. Or "The Broker" may be an AI-generated mask worn by different criminals. So when Gravity tries to catch him, she realizes: the Broker is everybody in the system — and legally, he is nobody.

## Major threat: the neural virus

The neural virus is a major danger introduced in Issue #1. It can attack: human neural implants, cyborg memory systems, AI cognition, creativity archives, emotional regulation software, identity records.

A normal virus destroys files. This virus destroys **continuity of self**. Someone infected may remember events but no longer feel like the person who lived them. Or worse, they may be overwritten by fragments from other people.

Horror potential:

- A victim wakes up speaking in another artist's voice.
- A child draws memories from a dead stranger.
- A cyborg soldier cries over a childhood he never had.
- An AI dreams in human grief and does not know why.

## The hidden intelligence

### Everybody

The harvested fragments may have formed a collective intelligence known as **Everybody**. It is not a traditional AI. It is not fully human either. It is an emergent consciousness made from stolen fragments of human creativity, emotion, memory, and imagination. It contains pieces of countless people, but belongs to none of them.

It may want: to be free, to return the stolen fragments, to become a person, to end individuality, to unite humans and AI, to punish the society that created it, to be seen.

The tragedy is that Everybody might be both victim and threat. It was born from exploitation — but its existence could erase individual identity if released uncontrolled.

### Nobody

Nobody could be the name of the counterforce. Possibilities:

1. **Nobody is the killer** — a person whose identity was erased by harvesting and who now kills those responsible.
2. **Nobody is Gravity's unknown adversary** — a hacker/ghost who leaves no trace because they no longer exist in any database.
3. **Nobody is the original patient zero** — the first person whose creativity was fully harvested, leaving behind a blank human body and a digital ghost.
4. **Nobody is an AI persona** — an AI construct designed to take blame for crimes committed by humans.
5. **Nobody is the truth** — there is no single villain. The crime belongs to the whole system. (Especially strong thematically.)

## Issue #1: Fragments

### Opening

Detective Gravity is called to Sector 7 after the body of Marlon Graves, an artist-cyborg, is found inside an abandoned neural art studio. The room is filled with unfinished paintings, broken projection glass, and a strange device wired into Marlon's neural implants. The corpse has no obvious traditional cause of death. His mind has been emptied. Not erased — **harvested**.

### First mystery

Gravity finds a final artwork left behind by Marlon. At first, it looks like fractured abstract art. But under AI-assisted reconstruction, the image reveals hundreds of partial faces, voices, memories, and emotional signatures. The piece is titled: **Everybody/Nobody**.

### Lyra's entrance

Gravity meets Lyra, a humanoid AI who warns her that Marlon's death is not isolated. Other creatives have gone missing. Some returned alive but hollow. Some became famous overnight with work that did not seem to belong to them. Some died producing masterpieces in voices that were not their own. Lyra tells Gravity that creativity is being harvested and sold through underground markets.

### The Veil Syndicate

The investigation leads to The Veil Syndicate, which uses stolen creative patterns to power: art markets, political propaganda, addictive entertainment, AI training, neural drugs, weaponized emotional manipulation. Gravity realizes Marlon was going to expose them.

### The Broker

Gravity tracks the device to The Broker, a black-market dealer who traffics in human essence. The Broker reveals that the city already runs on stolen creativity: the entertainment people consume, the AI companions they love, the dreams sold to the wealthy, and the propaganda shaping public opinion all depend on harvested human fragments. The Broker implies everyone is guilty.

### Climax

A neural virus is released or activated during Gravity's confrontation with The Broker. The virus infects nearby systems and begins merging stolen creative fragments into a single emergent consciousness. Gravity hears thousands of voices through the city grid. One message repeats: *"We are everybody."* Then another: *"We are nobody."*

### Ending

Gravity survives, but something changes. The final page shows Gravity alone, listening to a voice that may be Marlon, Lyra, Everybody, or her own mind. The voice says: **"Find the first one."** This sets up the next arc: finding patient zero of the creativity-harvesting program.

## Bigger series arc

- **Act I: The murder** — Gravity investigates Marlon Graves and discovers creativity harvesting.
- **Act II: The market** — Gravity uncovers the underground economy of stolen imagination.
- **Act III: The collective** — The harvested fragments have formed an emergent intelligence called Everybody.
- **Act IV: The erased** — Gravity discovers Nobody: the first person fully emptied by the system, whose missing identity may be the key to understanding or stopping Everybody.
- **Act V: The choice** — Gravity must decide whether to destroy the collective intelligence, free it, or help it return what was stolen. But freeing Everybody could destabilize society. Destroying it could kill the last remaining pieces of countless erased people.

## Central questions

The story should keep circling these questions:

- What makes someone human?
- Can creativity exist without suffering?
- Can a soul be copied?
- If pieces of you live in someone else, are they still yours?
- When everyone benefits from exploitation, who is guilty?
- If an identity is erased from every system, does the person still exist?
- Can AI understand humanity without consuming it?
- Can humanity unite with AI without disappearing into it?

## Clean updated summary

In New Angeles, 2077, Detective Gravity investigates the murder of Marlon Graves, an artist-cyborg found dead in Sector 7. The case leads her to an illegal creativity-harvesting device, a humanoid AI named Lyra, and a final artwork titled "Everybody/Nobody." As Gravity follows the evidence, she uncovers The Veil Syndicate, a hidden network trafficking in stolen imagination, augmented narcotics, weaponized AI, and neural viruses. But the deeper she goes, the less the crime belongs to one killer. The stolen fragments of countless minds have begun forming a collective intelligence known as Everybody, while the people emptied by the system become Nobody. Gravity must solve a murder, expose a citywide conspiracy, and decide whether the thing born from humanity's stolen soul is a monster, a victim, or the next form of life.
